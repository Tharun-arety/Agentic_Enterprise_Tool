# **Architecting the Product Data and Engineering Change Management Infrastructure for Advanced Magnetocaloric Refrigeration Systems**

## **Executive Overview**

The transition from academic research and laboratory-scale deep-technology development to commercial hardware manufacturing requires a fundamental restructuring of how product data, engineering designs, and manufacturing processes are managed. For an enterprise pioneering solid-state magnetic cooling—relying on the magnetocaloric effect of Lanthanum-Iron-Silicon (LaFeSi) alloys rather than conventional vapor-compression cycles—the complexity of the product architecture demands a highly disciplined approach to Product Lifecycle Management (PLM) and Product Data Management (PDM)1. The implementation of a robust Bill of Materials (BOM) architecture and a formalized Engineering Change Management (ECM) system is not merely an administrative exercise; it is a strategic imperative that directly influences supply chain resilience, manufacturing scalability, and product quality.  
As product portfolios expand from 100W commercial beverage coolers, which have already seen initial commercial deployment at scale, to 1kW commercial chillers and \+125kW industrial systems, the underlying digital thread must support concurrent engineering, rapid iteration, and stringent configuration control3. Furthermore, active development in cryogenic hydrogen liquefaction introduces entirely new data paradigms involving superconducting magnets and extreme temperature gradients1. This report provides a comprehensive, expert-level architectural blueprint for designing and implementing the PDM, BOM, and ECM frameworks required to support the commercial scaling of magnetocaloric cooling technologies. By anchoring the proposed frameworks in recognized industry standards such as ISO 10007 for configuration management, DIN 199 for BOM structures, and VDI 2219 for PLM implementation, the analysis delineates a highly structured pathway to operationalize these digital systems, ensuring that design intent is perfectly preserved from the CAD screen to the manufacturing floor.

## **The Magnetocaloric Technological Paradigm and Data Architecture Implications**

To properly design a PDM and BOM structure, the fundamental physical and functional architecture of the hardware must be meticulously analyzed. Magnetocaloric refrigeration diverges completely from the century-old gas compression methodology that relies on compressors, evaporators, condensers, and synthetic hydrofluorocarbon (HFC) or natural refrigerants like propane (R290)1. Instead, the system utilizes a solid-state Active Magnetic Regenerator (AMR) module, magnetic field generators, and a fluid transfer medium1.  
The engineering data model must account for distinct, highly specialized subsystems that interact continuously. The primary active component is the LaFeSi magnetocaloric material, which exhibits the thermodynamic property of heating up when magnetized and cooling down when demagnetized1. The structural integrity of this material is maintained through a proprietary anti-corrosive metal coating, representing a critical manufacturing and processing data point that must be captured within the digital product record1. The magnetic field is generated by permanent magnets—specifically Neodymium-Iron-Boron (NdFeB) arrays that exclude heavy rare earth elements to mitigate exposure to international export restrictions—or, in the case of cryogenic hydrogen liquefaction projects, advanced superconducting magnets2. A hydraulic subsystem, operating at low pressure (less than 1 bar), utilizes standard pumps to circulate a water-ethanol fluid through the porous AMR module to either absorb heat or deliver the cooling effect to the end application1. Finally, an electrical and control subsystem dictates the rotational sequencing of the magnetic forces, a synchronized mechanism that allows the recovery of work and increases overall cycle efficiency by up to 30% compared to traditional alternatives, achieving a 15% energy efficiency advantage over comparable propane systems1.  
The implication for the PDM infrastructure is that the product definition spans multiple engineering disciplines. Mechanical engineering (MCAD) governs the structural chassis, the containment of the magnetic field, and the hydraulic routing7. Electrical engineering (ECAD) governs the motor, pump controls, and sensor arrays9. Materials science defines the metallurgical composition of the LaFeSi alloys and coatings, while software engineering develops the sequencing control logic1. A single source of truth must orchestrate the convergence of these disparate data types into a unified product record7.  
The regulatory landscape also heavily influences the required data architecture. Because the system operates oil-free at atmospheric pressure with non-toxic, non-explosive materials, it is exempt from standard DIN EN378 regulations that govern conventional high-pressure and flammable refrigerants3. Furthermore, the exclusion of hydrofluoroolefins (HFOs) ensures the cooling cycle is entirely free of per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) and possesses zero direct Global Warming Potential (GWP)2. This regulatory freedom shifts the primary focus of the ECM system from compliance auditing—which dominates traditional refrigeration PLM configurations—to performance optimization, modularity, and supply chain management. The data architecture must trace these sustainability claims down to the component level, proving to commercial partners that the product remains environmentally benign across its lifecycle.

## **Structural Segregation of the Bill of Materials (DIN 199\)**

A frequent and often catastrophic failure mode in hardware commercialization is the reliance on a single, monolithic Bill of Materials. As physical products transition from the computer-aided design environment to the shop floor, the BOM must mutate to serve different operational audiences with competing priorities. Following the principles outlined in standard DIN 199, which provides the foundational definitions for technical product documentation, variant management, and BOM structures, the product structure must be segregated into distinct, purpose-built views13.

### **The Computer-Aided Design (CAD) BOM**

The CAD BOM is native to the design engineering environment and represents the geometric and spatial relationships of the physical product10. Structured in a top-down, hierarchical manner, it reflects how assemblies and subassemblies physically fit together in three-dimensional space10. For a magnetic cooler, the CAD BOM would contain the physical geometry of the AMR module housing, the exact spatial alignment and tolerances of the NdFeB magnets, and the external sheet metal or injection-molded chassis1.  
However, the CAD BOM is inherently incomplete; it operates as a skeletal framework rather than a comprehensive product definition. It typically lacks non-modeled items such as software firmware for the rotational sequence controller, the precise volume of the water-ethanol transfer fluid, industrial adhesives, packaging materials, and specific proprietary coating instructions10. Furthermore, CAD BOMs are highly dependent on the integration capabilities of PDM systems, which manage file check-ins, check-outs, and version control specifically for mechanical designers7. Relying on the CAD BOM for procurement or manufacturing inevitably leads to missing components and delayed production schedules.

### **The Engineering Bill of Materials (EBOM)**

The EBOM serves as the definitive record of the product "as designed" by the engineering organization16. It bridges the gap between raw mechanical design and holistic systems engineering, providing a functional and logical relationship of all components required to fulfill the product's requirements10. The EBOM consumes the mechanical assemblies from the CAD BOM, but augments them with the electrical components (printed circuit boards, wiring harnesses derived from ECAD tools like Altium), software configurations, and raw material specifications2.  
In advanced PLM architectures, the EBOM is heavily version-controlled and serves as the primary artifact managed by the engineering teams16. It answers the theoretical question of what the product is supposed to be, irrespective of the physical constraints or routing logic of the manufacturing facility16. For hardware operating in the sustainable technology sector, the EBOM is also the primary mechanism for tracking sustainability metrics. If a client requires proof that a Polaris unit contains no PFAS and possesses zero GWP, the EBOM provides the audited list of all sub-components and materials that validate that claim3. The EBOM is where the specific metallurgical formulation of the LaFeSi alloy is cataloged, ensuring that the correct material properties are linked to the theoretical performance models2.

### **The Manufacturing Bill of Materials (MBOM)**

While the EBOM defines the product as designed, the MBOM defines the product "as built"16. The transition from the EBOM to the MBOM is arguably the most critical digital handoff in hardware manufacturing, effectively translating theoretical design intent into operational execution7. The MBOM restructures the functional logic of the EBOM to align with the chronological assembly process on the shop floor. It is organized by plant, production line, area, and specific workstation10.  
The MBOM introduces manufacturing-specific elements that are entirely irrelevant to the design engineer but vital to the production planner:

* **Consumables:** The exact volumetric measurement of the water-ethanol transfer medium injected into the hydraulic system during final assembly1.  
* **Routings and Work Instructions:** The sequential steps required to apply the supplementary metal coating to the iron-based materials to prevent corrosion, including curing times and temperature parameters1.  
* **Phantom Assemblies:** Groupings of components that exist solely for the convenience of the assembly line (e.g., a "pump and hose sub-kit") but do not exist as independent subassemblies in the engineering design19.  
* **Packaging:** Materials required for shipping commercial units to retail environments, such as the customized vinyl wraps applied to Polaris units for brand marketing at events11.

Attempting to merge the EBOM and MBOM into a single structure leads to severe organizational friction10. Engineering teams require revision independence to iterate on tolerances or material properties, while manufacturing teams require structural independence to optimize assembly sequences without impacting the core design data10. ## **Configuration Management Framework (ISO 10007\)**

The realization of complex physical products, particularly those scaling from laboratory concepts to global retail environments, requires absolute certainty regarding product configurations. ISO 10007 (Quality management — Guidelines for configuration management) provides the definitive international standard for ensuring product identification, traceability, and accurate information access throughout the product lifecycle23. Implementing ISO 10007 principles ensures that the interrelated functional and physical characteristics of the magnetocaloric cooling units are meticulously recorded and protected against undocumented alterations23.  
The ISO 10007 framework comprises five primary disciplines that must be engineered into the operational PLM infrastructure:

### **1\. Configuration Management Planning**

This initial phase establishes the governing rules, software tools, and organizational responsibilities for managing product data23. It defines the naming conventions, the storage protocols in the PDM system, and the baseline requirements for initiating a change26. For a deep-tech startup, this planning phase is critical for ensuring that early-stage research data is properly structured so that it can eventually transition into a commercial product record26.

### **2\. Configuration Identification**

This discipline involves selecting and defining the Configuration Items (CIs) that satisfy an end-use function23. A CI is an entity within a configuration that is designated for separate configuration management. For instance, the entire 1kW Eclipse cooling platform represents a top-level CI3. Sub-tier CIs would include the AMR module, the specific permanent magnet array, and the pump and control board1. Selection criteria for CIs factor in regulatory requirements, life-cycle impact, and the integration of new or modified technology24. Properly identifying CIs allows engineers to track the history of specific, highly complex components—such as the LaFeSi alloy batches—without being overwhelmed by tracking generic, off-the-shelf fasteners.

### **3\. Change Control**

Change control is the formal mechanism used to evaluate, coordinate, and authorize modifications to a baselined Configuration Item23. It prevents unauthorized or undocumented changes from entering the production line23. If a thermal engineer discovers that adjusting the fluid transfer medium's ratio of water to alcohol prevents freezing more effectively at lower ambient temperatures, that change cannot simply be emailed to the shop floor; it must pass through a strict change control process1.

### **4\. Configuration Status Accounting**

This involves the formalized recording and reporting of the current configuration information, the status of proposed changes, and the status of the implementation of approved changes23. Status accounting provides real-time visibility into the precise state of a product. If a deployed Polaris unit requires maintenance, status accounting allows the service technician to look up the serial number and know exactly which iteration of the control board and which type of field generator was installed in that specific unit1.

### **5\. Configuration Audit**

The final discipline is the validation that the physical product precisely matches its digital configuration baseline and performance requirements23. In scaling operations, auditing is divided into two parts. A Physical Configuration Audit (PCA) verifies that the built product conforms perfectly to the technical documentation and the approved MBOM28. A Functional Configuration Audit (FCA) verifies that the product achieves its performance metrics—for example, proving that the Eclipse unit genuinely delivers 1kW of cooling capacity and maintains the 15% efficiency advantage over propane systems4.

## **The Engineering Change Management (ECM) Dynamics**

Engineering Change Management operates as the active, transactional engine within the broader configuration management framework. It is the formal procedure that controls how modifications to baselined product definitions are proposed, evaluated, approved, implemented, and documented28. In a fast-paced hardware scale-up environment, informal changes—such as substituting a micro-controller due to supply chain shortages or altering the thickness of the anti-corrosive coating based on new lab results—can trigger catastrophic supply chain misalignments or field failures if not systematically managed29.  
A best-in-class ECM process for a deep-tech manufacturer operates through a strictly governed workflow comprising three primary artifacts: the Engineering Change Request (ECR), the Engineering Change Order (ECO), and the Engineering Change Notice (ECN)28.

### **Phase 1: Engineering Change Request (ECR) and Risk Assessment**

The process initiates with an ECR, which documents a proposed modification to an established baseline28. An ECR may originate from internal R\&D (e.g., discovering that a slight variation in the rotational sequence of the magnetic forces yields a higher coefficient of performance) or from external supply chain pressures (e.g., sourcing magnets from decommissioned applications to enhance circularity and reduce costs)1. The ECR details the specific problem, the proposed solution, the affected Configuration Items, and a preliminary assessment of the technical, cost, and schedule impact28.  
At this stage, the concept of "traceability" becomes paramount. High-functioning engineering change processes are not judged by the speed at which they approve requests, but by the thoroughness of their traceability29. The PLM system must automatically flag any test and validation records that depend on the configuration being altered29. If the LaFeSi alloy's formulation is tweaked to improve longevity, the system must immediately identify the thermodynamic test protocols that require re-validation before the new alloy can be pushed to production.

### **Phase 2: The Change Control Board (CCB) Review**

Once submitted, the ECR is routed to the Change Control Board (CCB). The CCB serves as the ultimate authority on whether a proposed change is viable, wielding cross-functional jurisdiction over the product's evolution31. The composition of the CCB is critical; it must include multi-disciplinary experts from Systems Engineering, Quality Assurance, Manufacturing/Operations, and Procurement to ensure that a change beneficial to one department does not inadvertently cripple another28.  
The CCB evaluates the ECR against strict criteria:

* **Technical Validity:** Does the change compromise the inherent safety (non-explosive, non-toxic) of the system? Does it maintain or improve the targeted cycle efficiency advantage? Does the new component interact safely with the magnetic field?3  
* **Risk Assessment:** How does the change impact the manufacturing process? Will it alter the hydraulic flow of the water-alcohol transfer medium through the porous structure of the AMR module? Does it require new tooling on the shop floor?1  
* **Supply Chain & Cost Impact:** Does the change necessitate the sourcing of new raw materials? How does it affect current inventory availability, supplier contracts, and lead times?31

### **Phase 3: Engineering Change Order (ECO) Execution**

Upon approval by the CCB, the ECR is converted into an Engineering Change Order (ECO). The ECO provides the formal, documented authorization to implement the change across the organization28. It dictates the specific modifications required across the digital thread, executing changes to the EBOM, CAD models, and technical specifications28.  
Within modern PLM environments, the execution of the ECO includes assigning "effectivity" dates, determining whether the change should be applied to the production line "as soon as possible" (to address a critical defect) or at a specific future date (to phase in a cost-reduction measure after existing inventory is depleted)28. The ECO encompasses the exact adjustments required, such as altering the quantity of existing components, adding new structural fasteners, or modifying the routing operations on the MBOM34. The ECO stage is where the EBOM is officially revised, generating a new version and establishing a new baseline configuration28.

### **Phase 4: Engineering Change Notice (ECN) and Verification**

Following the physical and digital execution of the ECO, an Engineering Change Notice (ECN) is issued. The ECN acts as a formal communication broadcast to all internal stakeholders and external partners—such as contract manufacturers or commercial integration partners like HAUSER, who assist in integrating the technology into commercial refrigeration cabinets21. It confirms that the final revision details are now in force and that the older configuration is officially deprecated and obsolete28. The cycle concludes with verification to capture evidence that the approved modifications were correctly implemented on the physical product28.

## **Technological Infrastructure Selection (PDM, PLM, ERP)**

Selecting the optimal software architecture is the most consequential decision in establishing a product data infrastructure. The market presents a spectrum of tools ranging from CAD-centric Product Data Management (PDM) systems to expansive, enterprise-grade Product Lifecycle Management (PLM) and Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) integrations7. For a hardware organization navigating rapid scale-up, the chosen systems must offer robust version control, seamless CAD integrations, automated BOM management, and strict ECM workflows, while aggressively avoiding the massive implementation overhead and rigidity associated with legacy monolithic systems.

### **PDM vs. PLM Delineation**

It is critical to distinguish between the capabilities and ambitions of PDM and PLM. PDM is inherently engineering-centric; it manages the native CAD data, versions design files, controls check-in/check-out procedures, and maintains the geometric assembly structure that the CAD system understands7. Its primary function is to prevent engineers from overwriting each other's work and to serve as a secure, version-controlled vault for proprietary design files. PDM has no operational ambitions outside of the engineering department7.  
PLM, conversely, acts as the overarching system of record for the broader product definition7. PLM governs the EBOM, the change history (ECOs), configurations, and the lifecycle state of the product from initial ideation through to disposal7. While most PLM platforms contain a PDM layer or integrate deeply with an external one, PLM explicitly extends across multiple disciplines, linking mechanical data with electrical components, software binaries, requirements, and compliance documentation7.

### **Evaluation of Modern Software Architectures**

For a modern hardware entity seeking rapid deployment, high capability, and flexibility, cloud-native and agile solutions offer significant advantages over traditional on-premise installations.  
**Duro PLM** Duro represents a modern, cloud-native PLM designed specifically for agile hardware startups and contract-manufacturing-heavy environments7. It focuses heavily on reducing implementation friction and integrating directly into engineering workflows9.

* **Architectural Strengths:** Duro provides exceptional out-of-the-box bi-directional integrations with leading CAD tools (such as SolidWorks for MCAD and Altium for ECAD)8. This capability allows engineers to manage releases, push sub-revisions, and generate unique Customer Part Numbers (CPNs) directly from the CAD interface, minimizing context-switching8. Duro excels in visual change management, utilizing color-coding to highlight differences between BOM revisions (additions, subtractions, modifications)30. It also features built-in supplier integrations to pull real-time pricing and lead time data from off-the-shelf parts distributors, aiding procurement efficiency9.  
* **Suitability:** Highly suitable for establishing a rigorous EBOM, automating ECO routing processes with standard approval templates, and centralizing component data without requiring extensive IT infrastructure or a six-month deployment timeline7.

**Bild** Bild is a specialized, cloud-native PDM/PLM platform built explicitly for mechanical engineers, emphasizing strong control over CAD files, versions, and visual design reviews37.

* **Architectural Strengths:** It acts as a hardware data hub, closing the gap between traditional on-premise PDM limitations and the need for modern, secure cloud accessibility38. It supports multi-CAD environments, including workflows for SolidWorks and Creo, and integrates AI-driven engineering tools to streamline data management and automate repetitive tasks38.  
* **Suitability:** Ideal if the immediate and most painful operational bottleneck is purely the synchronization, versioning, and collaborative review of complex mechanical CAD assemblies, serving as a highly effective, low-friction PDM layer37.

**OpenBOM** OpenBOM is a user-friendly, cloud-based platform specializing in real-time BOM management and collaboration, designed for modern hardware teams and supply chains18.

* **Architectural Strengths:** It is designed around the concept of a multi-level BOM and provides robust item and catalog management20. It offers flexible data models, custom views for different departments, and facilitates concurrent collaboration, functioning somewhat similarly to advanced spreadsheet applications but embedded with the relational logic and version control required for complex product data18.  
* **Suitability:** Excellent for rapid, cost-effective BOM structuring and seamless supply chain data sharing, though it may lack some of the deeply automated, structured ECO routing templates and rigorous change control matrices found in platforms like Duro33.

**Odoo PLM (ERP Integration Focus)** Odoo PLM takes a fundamentally different architectural approach by positioning PLM as a tightly integrated module within a broader Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) ecosystem42.

* **Architectural Strengths:** The defining advantage of Odoo PLM is its seamless, native interaction with Odoo Manufacturing (MRP), Inventory, and Purchasing modules43. When an ECO is approved and applied in Odoo PLM, the changes are instantly reflected in the manufacturing module, automatically updating the production MBOM and routing instructions34. Furthermore, inventory synchronizes in real-time, preventing the procurement of obsolete components, and purchase orders can be instantly adjusted based on component changes43. Odoo provides highly visual kanban boards for tracking ECO stages and managing specific quality control points within assembly steps34.  
* **Suitability:** If the strategic objective is to perfectly align the EBOM with the MBOM and entirely eliminate the data silos between engineering design and shop-floor execution, an ERP-integrated PLM like Odoo is exceptionally powerful42. This is particularly relevant if manufacturing is handled internally rather than through outsourced contract manufacturers.

### **Architectural Recommendation**

To support the mass production of commercial platforms like Polaris and Eclipse, while concurrently managing the highly complex, multi-year R\&D trajectory of industrial systems and hydrogen liquefaction projects, a two-tiered architectural approach is optimal.  
First, a robust CAD-integrated PDM tool (such as Bild or a native CAD provider’s cloud solution) must govern the raw mechanical and electrical files, ensuring pristine version control of the complex magnetic arrays and hydraulic models7. Second, the PDM must feed directly into a centralized PLM system (like Duro) that maintains the definitive EBOM and governs the cross-functional ECO process via strict CCB routing templates9. Finally, pushing the finalized EBOM from the PLM into an integrated ERP environment (such as Odoo) to automatically generate and manage the MBOM ensures a continuous, unbroken Digital Thread from theoretical design intent to physical realization and inventory procurement16.

## **Strategic Implementation Framework (VDI 2219\)**

The introduction of PDM/PLM systems and formalized ECM processes frequently encounters significant organizational resistance, as they impose structure on historically informal engineering practices. The standard VDI 2219 (Information processing in product development \- Introduction and operation of PDM systems) outlines a highly structured, risk-mitigated approach to implementing these enterprise systems45. Adhering to this framework ensures that the technology deployment aligns with business objectives and operational realities.

### **Phase 1: Process Analysis and Requirements Definition**

Before any software is configured or purchased, the existing workflows must be systematically mapped and analyzed48. For a deep-tech company, this involves analyzing how data currently flows from the R\&D laboratories to prototype assembly and eventually to commercial production. The requirements definition must capture the specific metadata needed for magnetocaloric systems, such as magnetic field strength variables, fluid dynamics specifications, and material tolerance thresholds1. Furthermore, the requirements must dictate the status transitions within the document lifecycle (e.g., progressing a part from "In Design" to "Prototype" to "Production" to "Obsolete")36.

### **Phase 2: Data Modeling and Standardization**

A PLM system is only as effective as its underlying data model15. Consistent part numbering is the foundational prerequisite for any functioning PDM/PLM environment16. A Customer Part Number (CPN) schema must be established that logically categorizes mechanical parts, electrical components, and consumables without relying on overly complex, "intelligent" numbering systems that inevitably break down as product lines expand36. The data model must also define the specific attributes managed for each item type, ensuring that engineering properties (for the EBOM) and manufacturing properties (for the MBOM) are housed in appropriate, discrete data fields17.

### **Phase 3: System Selection and Customization**

Utilizing the requirements gathered in Phase 1, the optimal software stack is selected. A guiding principle during this phase is to minimize customization in favor of adopting the out-of-the-box best practices embedded in modern PLM solutions9. Configurations should focus strictly on establishing the exact routing of the ECO approval templates, ensuring that the defined Change Control Board members are automatically notified when an ECR requires review35. Integration touchpoints—specifically the APIs connecting the ECAD/MCAD tools to the PLM, and the PLM to the ERP/MES—must be rigorously mapped and tested to prevent data loss across the digital seams8.

### **Phase 4: Phased Rollout and Organizational Change Management**

A resilient implementation plan dictates a phased, incremental rollout, avoiding overly optimistic timelines that attempt to overhaul the entire enterprise simultaneously50. The strategy should target "quick wins"—areas with the highest ratio of benefit to effort50. For example, the organization should initially implement strict PDM version control and automated EBOM generation for the mature Polaris (100W) product line, stabilizing the data for a product already produced at scale, before attempting to model the highly complex, evolving baseline of the industrial Stellar unit or the cryogenic demonstrator3.  
Organizational Change Management (OCM) is equally critical to the success of the software deployment. Engineering, research, and manufacturing teams must be trained not merely on the software interface, but on the disciplined principles of configuration management and the rationale behind the new processes44. The cultural shift from rapid, undocumented physical iteration in a lab setting to a structured, digitally traceable change process is challenging, but it is an absolute necessity to achieve quality control and supply chain stability as production volumes scale internationally.

## **Scaling Complexity: The Hylical Project Use Case**

The theoretical principles of BOM structuring, configuration management, and ECM become highly practical and vital when applied to cutting-edge research developments such as the Hylical project. Hylical aims to demonstrate an energy-efficient, compact hydrogen liquefaction system based on the magnetocaloric effect, targeting efficiency gains of 30% to 50% compared to conventional liquefaction methods5. The project seeks to scale the technology from a laboratory proof-of-concept (Technology Readiness Level 3\) to a prototype demonstration (TRL 5\) by utilizing a massive 19-tesla superconducting magnet to cool hydrogen to \-253 degrees Celsius following pre-cooling with liquid nitrogen5.  
In this R\&D scenario, the configuration baseline is extremely fluid and highly complex. The EBOM will undergo constant revision as consortium partners, including the Helmholtz-Zentrum Dresden-Rossendorf (HZDR) and SUBRA, iterate on the superconducting magnet designs, magnetic field orientations, and magnetocaloric cooling variables to reach the target energy demand of 8 kWh/kg and a liquefaction cost of less than 1.5 €/kg5.  
The ECM process here acts as the vital, immutable scientific record. Every modification to the superconducting magnet design, the LaFeSi alloy structure, or the fluid thermodynamics must be processed through an ECR, capturing the exact scientific rationale for the change and linking it to specific experimental outcomes5. Furthermore, because the project is co-funded by the European Union and the Clean Hydrogen Partnership across multiple member states, strict configuration management ensures that all international partners are iterating on the exact same version of the CAD models and system requirements, preventing costly divergence in research efforts5.  
As the project approaches TRL 5 and prepares to transition toward industrial deployment, the focus of the PLM system will shift toward establishing a preliminary MBOM. This data structure will be crucial for calculating the targeted Capital Expenditures (CAPEX) and Operating Expenses (OPEX) reductions of at least 20%, and for preparing the technical data packages necessary to replicate the pilot plant for a decentralized network of local liquid hydrogen production facilities52. The PLM infrastructure ensures that the complex intellectual property generated during this phase is securely version-controlled, auditable, and directly traceable to specific engineering experiments, paving the way for the commercialization of clean, storable energy for the heavy-duty transport sector52.

## **Strategic Conclusions**

The commercial viability of breakthrough hardware technologies—such as refrigerant-free magnetocaloric cooling and superconducting hydrogen liquefaction—relies as heavily on rigorous digital data management as it does on the underlying thermodynamics of the product. The deployment of a unified PDM vault, structured EBOM and MBOM architectures, and strict Engineering Change Management processes represents the central nervous system of a scaling manufacturing enterprise.  
By enforcing the separation of design intent (EBOM) from physical assembly parameters (MBOM) in accordance with DIN 199, and by adopting the rigorous change control and traceability standards of ISO 10007, organizations can prevent the data silos, supply chain misalignments, and undocumented modifications that routinely derail hardware scaling efforts. The strategic utilization of modern, cloud-native PLM platforms integrated with agile ERP environments facilitates a continuous digital thread, enabling concurrent engineering and ensuring that every stakeholder—from R\&D scientists modeling 19-tesla magnetic fields to procurement officers sourcing specific alloys—operates from a single, unassailable source of truth. Implementing these digital frameworks deliberately and systematically guarantees that as the physical products scale to transform the global cooling industry, the organizational capability to manage their complexity scales in tandem.
